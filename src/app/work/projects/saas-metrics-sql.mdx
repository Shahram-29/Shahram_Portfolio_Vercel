**Key finding.** The MRR bridge, retention and cohort tables are written in SQL, not pandas — because in most finance teams that layer lives in the warehouse.


## Why SQL rather than pandas

The MRR bridge, retention and cohort tables in this project are written as SQL, not as dataframe operations. That is a deliberate choice: in most finance teams the recurring-revenue reporting layer lives in the warehouse, and the person who can express a cohort table as a window function is more useful than the person who exports to Excel first.

It runs on DuckDB, so the whole thing is reproducible from a file with no warehouse to provision.

## Cohort retention

![Cohort retention](/images/projects/saas-cohort-retention.png)

The cohort view shows annual renewals as clear steps at months 12 and 24 — the shape you expect from annual contracts, and a useful check that the cohort logic is right before anyone reads conclusions off it.

## The MRR bridge

![ARR and MRR movements](/images/projects/saas-mrr-bridge.png)

New, expansion, contraction and churn, reconciling opening to closing MRR. Alongside it the project covers:

- **NRR and GRR** — net and gross revenue retention
- **CAC, LTV and payback period**
- **IFRS 15 deferred revenue** — the accounting view, not just the metrics view
- **A 36-month scenario model** with cash runway under different growth and churn assumptions

Nine unit tests, run on every push.

## An honest note on the data

**The company data is simulated.** The customers, subscriptions and cash balances are invented to exercise the calculations. What this shows is the modelling and the SQL, not a real company's performance.
