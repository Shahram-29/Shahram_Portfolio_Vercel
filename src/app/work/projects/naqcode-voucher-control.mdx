> Part of the NaqCoDE finance function, rebuilt on simulated data. This is the
> control I designed and ran in practice as Finance Associate: I raised the
> vouchers, a director approved them, the CEO released the payment.

## The control

```
prepare  ->  approve  ->  disburse
(finance)    (director)   (CEO)
```

Three people, three steps, and **no one person can move money alone**. That is
the entire point. It is the oldest control in accounting and it is still the
one that catches the most.

## Most of the module is refusals

A control that cannot say no is not a control. So the code is mostly the list
of things it will not allow:

- **Self-approval** — the preparer approving their own voucher
- **The preparer or the approver releasing the payment**
- **Disbursement before approval** — the payment gate re-checks the approval
  rather than trusting the earlier step ran
- **Approval above the director's PKR 100,000 authority** without the CEO
- **A voucher with no narrative** — it cannot be approved on its face if it
  does not say what the payment is for
- **Back-dating**, in either direction: approving before the voucher was
  raised, or paying before it was approved

## The one I would point at in an interview

**The approved amount is frozen at approval and re-checked at payment.**

Edit the figure after the director signs and it fails at the CEO gate:

```
V001: amount changed from PKR 25,000.00 to PKR 250,000.00
after approval — must be re-approved
```

Without that, the approval signature means very little. A director signs a form
saying 25,000 and the bank sends 250,000, and every document in the file still
looks correct. This is the difference between a control and a form.

## Evidence that it operated

`find_duplicate_payments()` catches the classic double payment — same payee,
same amount, inside thirty days.

`control_summary()` reports the **segregation rate**: the share of disbursed
vouchers where the preparer, approver and disburser were three distinct people.
That number is what an auditor actually asks for. Not that the control exists —
anyone can show you a policy document — but that it operated, on every payment,
across the year.

## Tests

Sixteen tests, and each one feeds the control the specific breach it exists to
stop. A control that is never fed its own failure case has not been tested; it
has only been observed not to complain.
