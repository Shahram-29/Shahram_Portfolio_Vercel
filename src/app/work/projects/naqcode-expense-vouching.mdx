> Part of the NaqCoDE finance function, rebuilt on simulated data. I built and
> matched twelve months of expense records against their receipts as Finance
> Associate.

## A different question from the policy check

A policy check asks whether a claim was **allowed** — within the category
limit, receipted, submitted in time. Vouching asks whether a document actually
**exists** behind the money that left.

They are not the same test, and an auditor puts the second one first. A claim
can be perfectly within policy and still have nothing behind it.

## The year

![Receipt coverage by month](/images/projects/pk-vouching-coverage.png)

**244 expense lines, 238 receipts on file, 99.1% of claimed value supported**,
18 exceptions to follow up.

Coverage is measured **by value, not by count** — and there is a test asserting
exactly that. Nine PKR 1,000 vouched lines against one PKR 91,000 unvouched
line scores **9%, not 90%**. A count-based metric reads 90% and hides the
entire problem, which is precisely the number a weak process would report.

## Four failures, four different problems

| Finding | What it means |
|---|---|
| A line with no receipt | Unsupported payment |
| A receipt nobody claimed | Cost possibly never recorded |
| Claim and receipt disagree | Over- or under-claim |
| **One receipt on two lines** | The same cost paid twice |

## Why the last one is the point

A receipt supporting two separate claims passes **every other check**. The
claim sits inside the category limit. The amount ties to a genuine document.
The approval trail is complete and properly segregated. Nothing in the monthly
reporting looks wrong.

The only way to find it is to match receipt numbers across the whole year and
notice that one appears twice. That is why the exceptions report lists
duplicates first, above mismatches and missing receipts — it is the finding
least likely to surface any other way.

## Tests

Eighteen tests, each feeding one specific way the match can fail: a missing
receipt, an unclaimed receipt, an over-claim, an under-claim, a receipt used
twice, a receipt dated months from its expense, and coverage measured on the
wrong basis.
