## The rule that surprises people

Ireland's R&D tax credit is **payable in cash**. That sounds like a straightforward benefit, and for cash flow it is. But under the OECD's Pillar Two rules, a credit paid out in cash is treated as **income** rather than as a reduction in tax — which changes the effective tax rate the top-up calculation is measured against.

![Top-up tax by R&D spend](/images/projects/ct-pillar-two.png)

For the example company, that treatment means **€1.10m of top-up tax instead of €3.04m**. The chart traces how the top-up moves as R&D spend changes — the relationship is not the one most people expect before they work it through.

## An honest note on the data

The **company is simulated** — the profits, R&D spend and group structure are invented. The **tax rules are real**, taken from Revenue guidance and the Pillar Two framework, and the tests check the calculations against worked examples.

This is a model of the rules, not tax advice, and not a return anyone should file from.
