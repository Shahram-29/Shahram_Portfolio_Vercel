> MSc Business Analytics, Dublin Business School. November to December 2025.

## The work

Classification on the bank-marketing dataset: which contacted customers go on
to subscribe to a term deposit.

- **Binary field mapping** and **dummy encoding** for job, marital status,
  education and contact channel
- **SMOTE** to rebalance the classes
- **Decision Tree, Random Forest and SVC**, each tuned with `GridSearchCV`
- Compared on **accuracy and confusion matrices**

## Why the confusion matrix, not just accuracy

This dataset is heavily imbalanced — the large majority of contacted customers
do not subscribe. A model that predicts "no" for everybody scores very well on
accuracy and is completely worthless, because it never identifies the customers
the campaign exists to find.

Reading the confusion matrix is what exposes that. It separates the two errors
that matter in opposite directions: calling somebody a subscriber when they are
not wastes a phone call; missing a genuine subscriber loses the deposit. Those
costs are not symmetric, and a single accuracy figure hides both.

## On SMOTE

Rebalancing with SMOTE addresses the same problem from the training side rather
than the evaluation side — the classifier sees enough of the minority class to
learn something about it, instead of finding that "always predict no" minimises
its loss.

Worth being clear about the limit: SMOTE generates synthetic minority examples
by interpolating between real ones. It helps the model learn a boundary, but it
does not add information that was not in the data, and the evaluation still has
to happen against the real class balance.
