**Key finding.** Balancing is not the same as reconciled. Three items sat on the statement and nowhere in the cash book; the check refuses to pass until each one is named.


> Part of the NaqCoDE finance function, rebuilt on simulated data. I reconciled
> the company bank accounts monthly from October 2024 to August 2025, and did
> the same work at Nawaz Construction before that.

## The question a reconciliation actually asks

Not "do the two balances match" — they almost never do, and that is not an
error. The question is whether every difference between them can be **named**.
Whatever is left unnamed is the thing worth looking at.

That distinction decides the whole design. A tool that just reports a gap is
useless; a tool that accounts for the gap line by line, and then refuses to
sign off on the remainder, is doing the job.

## Matching in two passes

**Reference first, then amount within a ten-day clearing window** — because a
cheque written on the 28th may not clear until the 3rd.

The order matters, and it has its own test. Two payments of the same value in
the same month will happily match each other if you go on amount alone; taking
exact reference matches first means a coincidental amount cannot steal a
reference that belongs to another line.

## Classifying what is left

Leftovers get sorted into three groups, because they are three different
problems:

- **Unpresented payments** — written in the books, not yet through the bank.
  Normal, and they reverse next month.
- **Deposits in transit** — lodged but not yet credited. Also normal.
- **Items on the statement that never reached the books** — bank charges,
  interest, a standing order nobody posted. **Not normal.** These need a
  journal, and they are the only category that represents a genuine error in
  the ledger.

`check()` refuses to pass a reconciliation while any of that third group
remains outstanding. That is the difference between a reconciliation that is
*balanced* and one that is *done*.

## Stale items

`stale_items()` flags reconciling items older than thirty days. An unpresented
cheque still outstanding after a month is usually lost, never sent, or already
replaced — each of which needs somebody to do something, and none of which
resolve on their own. Left alone they roll forward month after month and
quietly become permanent.

## Tests

Eight tests, including the two that matter most: that reference matching beats
a coincidental amount match, and that an item on the statement with no entry in
the cash book blocks sign-off rather than being silently absorbed.
