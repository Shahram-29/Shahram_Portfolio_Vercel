## The payroll bridge

![Payroll bridge](/images/projects/pk-payroll-bridge.png)

Contractual salary is not what reaches the bank. Across the simulated year:
**PKR 57.6m contracted**, 0.6m lost to unpaid absence, 0.9m added in overtime,
**6.4m withheld in income tax** and 0.1m in EOBI, leaving **51.6m paid**.

All twelve monthly registers reconcile — earned gross less deductions equals
net pay, per employee and in total — and the run refuses to continue if a month
does not tie. A payroll you cannot reconcile is a payroll you should not pay.

## The tax curve, and a table that checks itself

![Marginal and effective rate](/images/projects/pk-tax-rate-curve.png)

Pakistan's salaried slabs are purely marginal, so unlike Ireland's USC there is
no income at which earning one more rupee leaves you worse off. A test asserts
that at every slab boundary.

The more interesting test is on the slab table itself. The fixed amounts
(30,000 / 180,000 / 430,000 / 700,000) are not hard-coded a second time as
expected values — the suite recomputes each one from the marginal rates below
it. A typo in the table fails the tests instead of quietly mispaying everyone.

## Why a raise doubles the tax line

One employee gets a 35% raise in January. Their **monthly deduction rises 101%**
— it doubles — because section 149 withholding spreads the whole year's
liability across twelve months, and the raise moves them from the 15% slab into
the 25% one.

This is the question payroll gets asked every single time somebody is promoted,
and it is why the engine withholds on annual contractual salary rather than on
the month in hand.

## Attendance, and what it actually costs

![Unpaid absence by department](/images/projects/pk-attendance-cost.png)

| Department | Pay lost to unpaid leave | % of that team's payroll |
|---|---:|---:|
| Operations | 266,725 | 2.79% |
| Engineering | 131,386 | 0.66% |
| Sales | 89,120 | 0.65% |
| Finance | 75,930 | 0.53% |

Operations loses roughly four times the share the other teams do — a number a
manager can act on, where a headline absence *rate* would not have shown the
cost.

Paid leave does not reduce pay, and unpaid leave is pro-rated on **working**
days rather than calendar days. Getting that distinction wrong is one of the
most common payroll errors, so it has its own test.

## On the rules

Pinned to **Tax Year 2025** (Finance Act 2024) because that is the year I
worked. The Finance Act 2025 changed the slabs again for TY2026 — the 5% band
fell to 1% and the 15% band to 11% — so the slab table is versioned data rather
than constants scattered through the code.

Slabs cross-checked against PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Befiler and EY's
Finance Bill 2024 alert. A model of the rules, not tax advice.
