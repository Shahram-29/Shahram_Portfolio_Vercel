> Team project with Amjad Ali and Abdul Rehman, for Project Management for Business Analytics on the MSc Business Analytics at Dublin Business School. Delivered as a proposal and slide deck — a designed system with a worked model, not a deployed product.

## The problem it addresses

A small finance team with fifty-plus overdue invoices has no good way to decide who to chase first. Ageing buckets sort by *how late* an invoice is, which is not the same as *how likely it is to stay unpaid* — and chasing in the wrong order costs collections.

## The proposal

An LSTM trained on payment history to forecast, per customer, the probability of late payment on an open invoice. The output is not a score in a dashboard; it is a **prioritised worklist** — the order a credit controller should work through on Monday morning.

The design covers:

- **Automated receivables tracking**, so the ledger position is current without manual export
- **A late-payment forecast per customer**, from sequence history rather than a static ageing bucket
- **Integration points for Xero and Sage**, because a tool that needs its own data entry will not be used
- **A prioritised worklist** as the primary interface

## What I took from it

This was the project that connected my finance-operations background to the analytics side of the MSc directly — the reconciliation and AP work I had done at Nawaz Construction is exactly the process this is trying to improve.

It also led on to the [receivables cash forecast](/work/receivables-cash-forecast) in my Irish FP&A portfolio, which I built solo afterwards and backtested properly against held-out collections. PredictPay is the design; that one is the tested implementation of the same idea.

## Scope, honestly

This was coursework: a proposal, an architecture and a model, assessed as a project-management deliverable. It was not deployed against a real company's ledger, and the integration with Xero and Sage is designed rather than built.
