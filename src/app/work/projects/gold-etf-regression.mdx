> Applied Statistics and Machine Learning, CA2, MSc Business Analytics at Dublin Business School.

## What it does

Predicts a gold ETF's adjusted close from a 1,718-row, 23-feature dataset, comparing six regression approaches on the same split:

- **Linear regression** as the baseline
- **Ridge** and **Lasso** for regularisation
- **Support Vector Regression**
- **Decision Tree**
- **Random Forest**

Each is tuned with `GridSearchCV` and scored on **hold-out R² against 5-fold cross-validated R²**, with a final model-comparison chart.

## Why both scores

Reporting cross-validated R² alone flatters a model that has been tuned against the same folds; reporting hold-out R² alone is noisy on a dataset this size. Showing both side by side makes the gap visible, which is where over-fitting shows up first — the tree-based models in particular look better in cross-validation than they hold up out of sample.

The habit of comparing a tuned model against a plainly-specified baseline is the one I carried into my [dissertation](/work/crisis-volatility-lstm-shap), where the baseline won.
