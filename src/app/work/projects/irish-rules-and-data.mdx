## Irish payroll tax: two cliffs and a squeeze on low pay

![Marginal rate by salary](/images/projects/irish-marginal-rates.png)

A net-pay engine for 2026 — PAYE income tax, USC and Class A PRSI, including the PRSI rise from 4.2% to 4.35% on 1 October 2026. Checked against Revenue's 2026 worked examples and the Department of Social Protection's PRSI guide, with sixteen tests.

Plotting the marginal rate across the salary range shows things the headline rates hide:

- **Two cliffs.** At €13,000 the USC exemption ends and the next €100 of salary costs €82. At €18,300 PRSI starts and the next €100 costs €174.
- **A low-pay squeeze.** Between €20,000 and €22,044 the tapering PRSI credit pushes the marginal rate to 42.9% — nearly as high as the 47.2% paid at €55,000.

## FRS 102: a trial balance that has to tie up

![Profit bridge](/images/projects/frs102-profit-bridge.png)

Lays a trial balance out in the Companies Act 2014 Schedule 3 formats — a Format 1 profit and loss account and balance sheet, the notes, and eleven ratios with their formulas.

It has four tie-up checks and **refuses to write anything until the numbers reconcile**. Each check is tested by feeding it the specific error it is meant to catch, which is the only way to know a validation actually validates.

## Euro area yield curves from ECB parameters

![The AAA yield curve on four dates](/images/projects/ecb-curve-snapshots.png)

Rebuilds euro area yield curves from the ECB's published Svensson parameters and checks the result against the ECB's own published spot rates. Three things it shows:

- **Negative everywhere.** In March 2020, AAA governments were paid to borrow at every maturity out to 30 years.
- **Inverted.** In July 2023 the 2-year yield sat 0.64 points above the 10-year.
- **Draghi's 2012 speech** cut the all-government 2-year yield by 30bp in a single day, while barely moving AAA yields.

## Irish inflation against mortgage rates

![Irish CPI inflation and new mortgage rates](/images/projects/cso-inflation-mortgages.png)

Irish CPI from the CSO against new mortgage rates, 2003 to 2026, pulled live from the CSO and ECB APIs:

- **Slow pass-through.** The ECB raised rates by 4.5 points across 2022–23; new Irish mortgage rates rose by about 1.6.
- **Negative real rates.** Across 2022–2023, inflation averaged 7.1% against mortgage rates of 3.26%.
